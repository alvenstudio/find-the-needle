## How it was read

**"Exactly the same, but first person."** The reference link resolves to the
August 2026 wave of Roblox needle-in-a-haystack games. What they share — and
what this game keeps — is the loop: pull hay from a giant stack, carry a small
bag of it to a cow, sell, buy upgrades that make the next pull bigger, and
eventually turn up a needle. What they also share, and what took research to
establish rather than assume, is that a *run* is one haystack: cash and upgrades
die with it and only a second currency crosses the boundary. That structure is
the reason the shop stays interesting, so it is here too.

First person is not a reskin of a third-person game. It changes what the player
can do — lean into the face of the stack, sweep it with their eyes, tell one
crater from another — so the pile is a real height field you dig craters into
rather than a counter that ticks down, and the hay you are standing in front of
is made of actual straws.

**"Make the models yourself in Blender."** Every model in `public/models/` is
generated by Python in `tools/blender/`. There is no `.blend` file: a model is a
function, and changing the barn is a diff.

**"The game must be balanced."** Balance is measured rather than asserted.
`tools/balance.ts` imports the real tables and plays whole runs, including the
walking and the waiting, and every number in `Content.ts` was moved until that
report said what it should.

**Where it deliberately differs.** Three additions the reference games do not
have, each because first person makes it possible and the game is better for it:
a **magnifier** that widens the range at which buried oddities glint, a **Hunch**
that gives a heading rather than an answer, and **golden bundles** that reward
looking around instead of staring at one spot. None of them find the needle for
you — that was measured too, and a sense that touches the needle collapses the
search into a minute.
